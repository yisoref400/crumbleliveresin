  <h2>🧪 Why Fresh-Frozen Extraction Actually Matters</h2>

  <p>Terpenes are the aromatic compounds responsible for a strain's smell and flavor, and they're notoriously fragile. Heat, light, and time all break them down, which is a problem for traditional concentrates made from plant material that's already spent days or weeks drying and curing.</p>

  <p>Live resin sidesteps that decay by freezing the plant right after it's cut, often within a couple of hours. That means the terpene profile stays much closer to what it was on harvest day. Concentrate fans often say live resin products smell and taste noticeably more accurate to the strain than anything made from cured material, and that difference isn't just marketing. It comes down to basic chemistry: fewer degradation steps mean more of the original compounds survive.</p>

  <p>There's also a functional side to this. Terpenes interact with cannabinoids to shape the overall effect of a product, sometimes called the entourage effect. A richer terpene profile can mean a more rounded, complete experience compared to a concentrate where most of those compounds have already faded.</p>

  <h2>⚙️ How Crumble Live Resin Is Made</h2>

  <p>The process starts in the field. Fresh cannabis plants are flash-frozen shortly after harvest, sometimes within just a couple of hours, and stay frozen all the way through transport and storage. This is the defining step that separates live resin from most other concentrate categories.</p>

  <p>From there, extractors use a solvent, typically butane or propane, to pull cannabinoids and terpenes out of the frozen plant material. What comes out at this stage is a sticky, unrefined extract that still contains leftover solvent and needs further processing before it's usable.</p>

  <p>The final step is purging, done under carefully controlled temperature and pressure. This is what actually creates the crumble texture, removing residual solvent and moisture in a way that causes the extract to dry out and flake apart instead of staying soft or hardening into glass. Small shifts in timing or heat during this step can push a batch toward a completely different texture, which is part of why skilled processing makes such a noticeable difference in the final product.</p>
